De hac editioneAbout this edition

Martianus Minneius Felix Capella wrote De nuptiis Philologiae et Mercurii in the fifth century AD, probably at Carthage. Books I and II tell the betrothal and Philology's ascent; Books III to IX are the bridesmaids' speeches: Grammar, Dialectic, Rhetoric, Geometry, Arithmetic, Astronomy and Harmony. The work was a standard schoolbook for much of the Middle Ages.

The translation is made from the Latin text of James Willis (Teubner, 1983), as transcribed in the Bibliotheca Augustana. Each passage was drafted, checked against the Latin by an independent reviewer, revised and checked again; the whole was then read through against the Latin, and the references in the notes were checked. A number of doubtful places were compared with the English version of Stahl, Johnson and Burge (1977). The translation was made by Claude, an AI model, and has not been reviewed by a human Latinist; use it with that in mind.

The standard section numbers, 1 to 1000, run through the text, so any passage can be found in the Latin or in other translations. Daggers (†) mark corrupt words, angle brackets (⟨ ⟩) words supplied by an editor, and three asterisks a gap in the text. About 790 notes explain allusions, what is lost in translation, and what is not evident from the text.
